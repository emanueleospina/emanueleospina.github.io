Pricing lessee credit risk when fuel drives everything

A mid-life A320ceo with five years left on its lease. One version is leased to IAG (BBB). The other to Volotea, which entered pre-insolvency in September 2026 citing jet fuel. Same aircraft, higher rent. Is the premium worth it?

Emanuele Ospina · November 2026 · Illustrative analysis from public data, not investment advice
01

The rent premium hides the risk

Volotea's median return beats IAG's, but its worst 5% of outcomes are far worse. Similar averages, very different risk.

02

Fuel correlates the risks

A fuel shock raises default risk, cuts re-lease rents, lengthens downtime and lowers residuals at once. Modelling them separately understates the tail.

03

The fix is the price, not the rent

No carrier in restructuring signs the rent needed to match IAG's tail. A purchase discount does it.

Why fuel, and why now

European jet fuel roughly doubled after the Strait of Hormuz disruption in early 2026, and most carriers were hedged only into summer 2026. Ryanair has warned that European fares could rise 10–20% in 2027 and that weaker airlines may fail. Volotea filed for pre-insolvency protection in Barcelona on 29 September 2026, citing an estimated €150M fuel impact, and plans to shrink from 44 to 30–35 A320s.

For a lessor, that is credit risk and asset risk arriving together: aircraft come back exactly when more of them are coming back.

Method

Monthly cash flows over 60 lease months, 8,000 paths per lessee. Each path draws one fuel scenario that sets default probability, re-lease rent, downtime and residual value together. On default, rent stops, a repossession and transition cost is paid (median $1.0M), the aircraft sits idle, then is re-leased at a lower rent. Maintenance reserves are pass-through; returns are unlevered. The 20,000-path Python model behind the PDF gives the same results within sampling noise.

Anchors and limits

  • $17M at $215k/month implies a ~1.26% lease rate factor, consistent with IBA's ~$220k rent for a 12-year-old A320ceo (Q3 2026).
  • Default rates follow S&P long-run studies by rating; IAG is BBB (S&P, Fitch) and Baa3 (Moody's).
  • Remarketing: most lessors re-place aircraft in 3–6 months, a quarter take 6–12 (industry survey).
  • Scenario probabilities are judgement. Appraiser data, deposits and letters of credit, and leverage are natural extensions.
